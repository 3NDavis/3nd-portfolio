Shooting stars was made for a 2 week game jam with the theme "positivity".<br><br>
 
 We also had one additional goal which was to showcase everyone's individual skills and how much we can do in 2 weeks. We were a team of 8:
 - 3 environment artists
 - 1 2D Artist (concept, splash art, UI)
 - 1 Character Artist
 - 2 Designers/VFX Artists
 - Me as the sole programmer, and sometimes a designer.
 The hardest of these to showcase was the character artist. Not having someone who could animate we decided to make a first-person game, but that still begged the question of what are character artist could do. We landed on Statues.
## Week 1
This project moved incredibly fast, within the first day we decided on the "fixing constellations" as our gameplay loop. This led to a crossbow and the perspective puzzle, both of which I had prototypes for within the next 3 days. While I was working on that, the visuals and narrative were being decided on, the level and terrain were being boxed out. We had a greybox, and proxy models of every asset within the first week. 

## Week 2
The second week had us replacing the greyboxes with finalized art, adding water effects, fog and particle effects, adding the UI, and a general polish overhaul. 

# Post Week 2
The game jam finished at the end of week 2, and we got feedback on our work either directly or by watching others.

From my perspective there were 2 main pitfalls of the project
1. There was no audio.
2. We didn't explain the perspective puzzle very well, players figured out they had to shoot the stars into the sockets, but not what to do afterwards. 

I went back into the project that week and added in audio and it made a huge difference. just the music and ambient wind alone made everything feel much more cozy. 

The perspective puzzle issue was one that required a much more intense fix since it wasn't a: "missing content or fix a bug" it was a QA issue where we would have to try something and test it with players. 

# Future
What we managed to accomplish in those 2 weeks was pretty incredible to me, and we occasionally talk about some stuff we didn't get to. We wanted the animals to move, we wanted the tree in the middle to grow. We wanted the player to be able to move the stars once they had been placed into a socket. This project definitely still has some legs.
